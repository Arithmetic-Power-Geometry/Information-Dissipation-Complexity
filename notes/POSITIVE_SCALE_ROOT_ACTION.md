# Positive-scale action on roots

If information gaps are multiplied by (a>0), then central cumulants satisfy
[
kappa_{3,a}(t)=a^3kappa_{3,1}(at)
]
when multiplicities and normalized gap ratios are fixed. Hence positive roots transform as
[
t_j(a)=t_j(1)/a.
]

Consequences:

1. The total number of positive-(t) roots is invariant under common positive scaling.
2. The number of physical roots (t>1) is not invariant, because scaling moves roots across the fixed boundary (t=1).
3. Scale optimization can reveal positive roots that were previously below the physical threshold, but it cannot create new positive roots.
4. To improve the total positive-root count from five to six or seven, one must change normalized geometry and/or multiplicity ratios so that new roots are born, typically through a multiple-root event satisfying (kappa_3=kappa_4=0).

This lemma explains both why retaining scale was necessary to discover the certified five-root physical example and why scale variation alone cannot push that same normalized family beyond its five positive roots.
